# Where our CRM trails Jobber's depth

Our CRM is already deeper than Jobber where a Calgary snow crew earns its money (storm operations, Interac e-Transfer matching, texting, signed quote approval), and shallow in the plumbing around it: records cannot be edited after creation, the price book is nearly read-only, nobody but an owner can safely log in, routes ignore roads, and clients cannot order anything themselves. The inventory rates snow storms and e-Transfer matching 5 out of 5, but the price book 2, team and permissions 1-2, and ordering 0-1. The fastest gains are not new modules. They are about a dozen small fixes the code audit surfaced: an unused `CREW` role, seasonal and per-event snow billing that exists in code but cannot be reached, a `/book` form that never sends the time slot the API supports, quotes that never compute GST, and jobs and invoices that cannot be edited. After those, three builds carry most of the value: card on file with auto-charged seasonal plans, road-distance routing with per-day overrides and ETAs, and a self-serve ordering flow in the client hub. Jobber has no purchase orders or inventory, no native e-Transfer collection, no storm object, no client self-reschedule and no late-fee engine, so several of the things we lack are not parity gaps at all. Warehouse and truck inventory, supplier integrations, multi-list price books and a general workflow builder are not worth building for a crew this size. One caution on the evidence: the competitor research rests on vendor help centres and marketing, with no forum or Reddit testimony retrieved, and our own inventory is a code read with nothing run.

## How to read this audit

Our side of every comparison comes from `our_crm_inventory.md`, a read-only audit of `crm/` at commit `5bad8f4` dated 2026-11-28. Nothing was started or tested, so statements such as "the team board is likely desktop-only" or "queued photos can be lost on an expired session" are readings of code, not observed behaviour. Jobber's side comes from help.getjobber.com articles read through a summarising fetcher on 2026-11-27/28, so wording is paraphrased and exact limits should be re-checked before they go into a spec.

Each recommendation below is one of three kinds. **Parity** means Jobber does it and we do not. **Leapfrog** means Jobber is weak or absent and we are already ahead or can get ahead cheaply. **Skip** means it is not worth building for a small residential crew. Effort sizes in the roadmap (S, M, L) are this report's rough judgement from the inventory, not estimates from the developers.

The realistic comparison is not Jobber's top tier. The researchers judged that a two-to-four person crew would sit on Connect or Grow, where the US pricing page lists Connect at $139 to $199 a month and Grow at $199 to $299 month-to-month for one to five users, with Pipeline ($49), Marketing Suite ($99) and AI Receptionist ($99) as add-ons ([Jobber pricing](https://getjobber.com/pricing-plans/)). Crew scheduling, profit alerts and custom reports are Plus only ([The Plus plan](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-plus-plan/)). No Canadian-dollar price list was retrieved; the `/ca/` pricing page showed USD.

## CRM and sales pipeline: a rich contact record with no pipeline, import or merge

**What we have.** Clients carry multiple labelled emails and phones, extra contacts, multiple properties with gate codes, pets, hazards and driveway size, tags, linked notes, a call log pulled from Telnyx, an activity feed, lead source, CASL consent, a referral code and a running-balance statement. "Lead" is derived as "has no job yet". Requests have four statuses. Missing entirely: custom fields, CSV import and export, a merge tool, a duplicate warning on manual create, lead stages, lost reasons and follow-up tasks. Two stored fields do nothing: the `isBilling` contact flag is never used when sending invoices, and `emailBouncedAt` is read in many places but never written.

**What Jobber does in depth.** Jobber's lead is the same idea as ours, an automatic label that drops off at the first job, sent invoice or approved quote ([Lead management](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/lead-management/)). The depth sits around it. Billing contacts pre-populate on quotes and invoices and receive receipts ([Client basics](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/115009450867)). Typed custom fields apply to clients, properties, quotes, jobs, invoices and team members, with "transferable" fields flowing forward from quote to job to invoice ([Custom fields](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/custom-fields/)). CSV import handles about 5,000 rows, and re-importing an export bulk-updates existing clients ([Import clients](https://help.getjobber.com/es/articles/import-clients/)). A merge tool moves jobs, quotes, invoices, conversations and notes from a duplicate to a primary ([Merge duplicate clients](https://help.getjobber.com/es/articles/merge-duplicate-clients/)). UTM parameters on booking links auto-fill lead source, and a lead source report shows clients, quotes, jobs, revenue and conversion per source ([Lead management](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/lead-management/)). Requests carry nine statuses, most derived from the assessment's state ([Request basics](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/request-basics/)). The Sales Pipeline is a paid kanban (Plus or a US$49 add-on) where each request and quote is a card that advances automatically, with tasks, a salesperson, freshness chips and a manual Lost with an optional reason ([Sales Pipeline](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/34647017424023)).

**What competitors do beyond Jobber.** Housecall Pro's Pipeline ties a timed follow-up to every column: a lead sitting in First Contact gets a text and moves to Second Contact, later steps send text and email and then mark it Lost, and sent estimates are re-sent through three follow-ups and then auto-declined ([HCP: Getting started with Pipeline](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6185127-getting-started-with-pipeline)). It also shows a red "Do Not Service" label for flagged customers ([HCP fall 2025 product updates](https://housecallpro.com/resources/fall-2025-product-updates)). No source described lead scoring in any product.

**Recommendations.** Parity, in order of value: use the billing contact when sending invoices, receipts and reminders; add CSV import and export for clients; add a duplicate warning on manual create and a merge tool; capture UTM parameters and `?ref=` on every booking entry point and add a lead source report; add derived request states for "assessment overdue" and "assessment complete" with reschedule and complete actions on the request page. A board view over existing requests and quotes with a lost reason is worth doing after those, built the Housecall Pro way (a view over records we already have, with one default follow-up per stage) so it does not need a new "deal" object. Leapfrog: we already hold consent state and quiet hours for texting, so stage follow-ups by SMS are cheap for us, where Jobber's automation builder cannot act on requests at all ([Custom automation builder](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/custom-automation-builder/)). Custom fields are a larger build; for this crew a handful of fixed property attributes (corner lot, sidewalk length, steps, lawn area) delivers most of the benefit, and general custom fields can wait. Skip lead scoring and per-salesperson reporting.

## Quotes and price book: strong approval flow, missing tax, templates and catalogue editing

**What we have.** Quotes have optional lines with a live total, a flat-dollar discount, a default 30-day expiry, percent or fixed deposits that become a separate deposit invoice, drawn-signature approval with a document hash, IP and user agent, change requests, one follow-up at three days, and conversion to a one-off job. The gaps are concrete. **`quoteTotal` ignores tax and `Quote.taxCents` is never computed.** There are no templates, no duplicate, no percent discount, no SMS send, no decline button and no PDF. `depositRequired` is display-only. The quote page comment promises photos that are never rendered to the client. The price book has 19 seeded services, six with prices, and Settings can change only price and the active flag. The bookable service list is hard-coded to 14 labels in `validate.ts`.

**What Jobber does in depth.** Jobber offers up to 30 quote templates on all plans, linked by name to the catalogue so later price changes propagate ([Quote templates](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/quote-templates/)). Discounts are dollar or percent and carry to the job and its invoices; the tax rate is editable per quote ([Quote basics](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/quote-basics/)). Deposits can be a full payment schedule of named instalments, paid in one step with "Approve & Pay Deposit" ([Deposits on quotes](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/deposits-on-quotes/)). Two follow-ups are configurable up to 90 days out and go by the same channel as the quote ([Automations](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/automations)). "Create Similar Quote", send by text, and a per-quote history exist ([Quote basics](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/quote-basics/)). Line images, markup and margin are Grow features ([Quoting on the Grow plan](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/quoting-on-the-grow-plan/)). The catalogue itself is a single flat list with name, description, price, a tax-exempt flag and an active toggle, plus cost, markup, photo and online-booking duration and quantity on select plans; the researcher found no multiple price lists and no user-defined categories ([Products & Services list](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/115009735848)). Two points are unconfirmed: "Quote Options" (sets of two to five alternatives) is known only from a search snippet of a beta article that returned 404, and no Jobber article on quote expiry was found, so Jobber appears to rely on auto-archive rather than a valid-until date.

**What competitors do beyond Jobber.** Housecall Pro has side-by-side good/better/best layouts, deposits at approval and automatic conversion of approved estimates into jobs ([HCP fall 2025 product updates](https://housecallpro.com/resources/fall-2025-product-updates)). ServiceTitan attaches recommended add-ons to each service and carries four price slots per item (standard, member, add-on, member add-on) ([ServiceTitan Pricebook Pro guide](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/pricebook-pro-setup-guide-in-servicetitan-max.md)). Its dynamic pricing rules and supplier cost feeds are enterprise tooling.

**Recommendations.** Parity: compute GST on quotes (this is a correctness fix, not a feature); make the price book fully editable (add, rename, describe, categorise, cost, tax-exempt, default duration, bookable) and drive the booking service list from it instead of code; add quote templates, duplicate, percent discount, a decline button, SMS send and a second configurable follow-up; collect the deposit inline on the approval page and block conversion or scheduling when a required deposit is unpaid. Leapfrog: we already have quote expiry and tamper-evident signature records, which the Jobber pages read do not document. A cheap extra is a "recommended add-ons" link between price-book items, which also feeds self-serve ordering below. Skip multiple price lists, per-client price books, dynamic pricing rules, markup-by-cost-range engines and supplier catalogue sync. Good/better/best option sets are a later nice-to-have for cleanups and fix-up work; our optional lines already cover the residential case.

## Ordering: Jobber has no purchasing module, and nobody documents self-serve add-ons

**What we have.** For materials, an `Expense` table with seven fixed categories and a receipt attachment. Ice melt is a yes/no per visit that adds a fixed charge, with no quantity. For customer ordering, two priced add-ons on the booking form, one-click snow plan renewal, and a free-text "Need something else?" box in the hub that creates a request. Nothing in the schema models stock, purchase orders or suppliers. A client cannot pick a service, add-on or time from the hub, and cannot reorder past work.

**What Jobber does.** Less than the question assumes. The researchers found **no native purchase-order, supplier-ordering or inventory module** in Jobber's help centre. The nearest feature is supplier-invoice scanning on Plus (or a Bookkeeping add-on), which turns uploaded or forwarded supplier invoices into draft expenses and matches them to a job when the job number was written on the PO ([Automated job costing](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/41272823874967)). Materials otherwise live as a unit cost on catalogue items plus expenses ([Job costing](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/14343244961175)). Purchase orders and truck stock come from third parties such as Ply, per its own marketing ([Ply + Jobber](https://getply.com/jobber/)). The Home Depot catalogue integration is US only ([Jobber and the Home Depot integration](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-and-the-home-depot-integration)). The caveat here is real: Jobber's app marketplace returned 403 and was not browsed, so other purchasing apps may exist.

On the customer side, Jobber's booking forms let a client choose services with price and duration, pick a quantity within a minimum and maximum, and save a card, and they gate by postal code and drive time ([Online booking](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/online-booking/)). Online booking creates only assessments or one-off jobs, never recurring work, and no deposit at booking is documented. The client hub lets a client submit the default request form but nothing more structured ([What do your clients see in client hub?](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/what-do-your-clients-see-in-client-hub)).

**What competitors do beyond Jobber.** Workiz and FieldPulse track stock across warehouses and vehicles with low-stock alerts and purchase orders tied to the price book and jobs ([Workiz inventory](https://www.workiz.com/features/inventory-management/); [FieldPulse inventory](https://fieldpulse.com/features/inventory-management)). ServiceTitan adds supplier procurement and PO approval workflows ([ServiceTitan procurement](https://help.servicetitan.com/commercial/docs/full-procurement-integration-home)). Snow specialists log materials per visit rather than run inventory: CrewNest's Snow Log records material type and pounds per visit ([CrewNest](https://www.crewnest.app/snow-removal-crm)). For customer ordering, ZenMaid booking forms use a base price plus priced extras with a minimum booking price ([ZenMaid booking forms](https://zenmaid.com/answers/en/articles/3033642-how-to-create-edit-booking-forms-in-zenmaid)), and Deep Lawn produces an address-based quote with online checkout, by vendor claim in 60 to 90 seconds ([Lawn & Landscape](https://www.lawnandlandscape.com/news/service-autopilot-by-xplor-unites-with-deep-lawn)). The researcher found **no source describing a customer-facing self-serve add-on or upsell flow in any product surveyed**. That is an absence of evidence, not proof nobody has built one.

**Recommendations.** Skip warehouse and truck inventory, purchase orders, supplier records, supplier catalogue imports and PO approval. Jobber does not have them, and a shovelling crew buying ice melt and fuel does not need them. The parity-level materials story is small: a unit cost on price-book items, an ice-melt quantity (bags or kg) per visit instead of a yes/no, and vendor, GST split and edit on expenses. If stock ever matters, a single "bags on hand" counter with a low-stock nudge is the ceiling.

Self-serve ordering is where to invest, and it is a leapfrog, not parity. Three pieces, each building on code that exists: first, fix `/book` so it sends the slot, referral code, consent and coordinates the API already accepts; second, replace the hub's free-text box with a structured order (pick a service from the price book, choose the property, add photos, pick a slot); third, let a client add ice melt or an extra sidewalk to their plan, add a one-off extra to an upcoming visit, and reorder a past job. Our driveway-size pricing and price-approval link already do the hard part of address-based quoting, so a full satellite-measurement engine is unnecessary. Service-area rejection with a waitlist is a sensible companion, though the specialist researcher could not confirm waitlists or zone caps in any product.

## Jobs and recurring work: good completion logic, but records freeze at creation

**What we have.** Three job kinds (one-off, recurring, snow plan), visits with a six-state field machine, checklists with required items, before and after photos, a completion signature, per-visit timers, expenses and job profit. Completion blocks on required checklist items and a missing signature, and re-completing never double-bills. The weaknesses: **jobs are not editable after creation** (title, instructions, property, billing type and line items are read-only); the recurring form has two line rows and no assignees, times, checklist or billing choice; recurring billing is hard-coded to per-visit charges invoiced monthly; recurrence stops at weekly, biweekly and monthly; there are no per-visit line overrides, no duplicate job and no Cancel button on the visit page. In snow, **only per-push pricing is reachable**: `SEASONAL` and `PER_EVENT` are handled in `chargesForVisit` but nothing sets them, and `seasonalPriceCents` is unused.

**What Jobber does in depth.** A recurring job pairs a billing method (per visit, or fixed price per period regardless of visit count) with an invoicing schedule (month end, after each visit, on close, custom dates, or none) ([Create a recurring job](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/create-a-recurring-job/)). Recurrence includes custom weekly, monthly and annual patterns such as the first and third Friday. A single visit's line items can be edited without touching the job. "Edit All Visits" changes schedule, team, instructions and checklists for incomplete visits, and "Create Similar Job" copies a job ([Job basics](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/job-basics/)). Job status is derived, including "Requires Invoicing", "Action Required" and "Ending Within 30 Days" ([Jobs list page](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobs-list-page-and-key-metrics/)). Jobber's checklists only warn on unanswered required questions; the visit can still be completed ([Checklists](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/checklists/)). Jobber's snow workflow is one "as needed" recurring job per property per season, with a bulk "Create New Visits" tool capped at 100 jobs per run, depth tiers modelled as separate jobs and zones as client tags ([Snow removal workflow](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/36429203202071-Create-New-Visits-for-Existing-Recurring-Jobs-Snow-Removal-Workflow-New-Schedule)). The researcher found no pause, skip, season or storm concept, and job cost reporting is not available for recurring jobs ([Job costing](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/14343244961175)).

**What competitors do beyond Jobber.** Snow tools mix billing models across one customer base: SnowBossPro supports per-push, per-inch, per-event and seasonal billing ([Upper roundup](https://www.upperinc.com/blog/best-snow-removal-business-software.md)), and CrewNest offers fixed monthly, per-push or per-inch tiers ([Icey comparison](https://iceysoftware.com/blog/best-snow-removal-software-2026)). Seasonal contracts are commonly paid in monthly instalments from November to March ([Agiled](https://agiled.app/blog/best-contract-software-for-snow-removal)). Housecall Pro service plans auto-charge a saved card monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annually, email the customer on failure and list plans "Due for Billing" ([HCP service plans](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/4530621-billing-and-payments-for-service-plans)). LMN shows crews actual against estimated time at every clock-out ([LMN snow](https://golmn.com/industries/snow)). No product page described contract caps with overage, a "city plough came back" return visit, or an end-of-season renewal flow; those are unconfirmed everywhere.

**Recommendations.** Parity: make jobs editable; bring the recurring form up to the one-off form (assignees, times, checklist, billing choice, more lines); add every-N-weeks and custom recurrence; allow per-visit line and price overrides before invoicing; add duplicate job and a visit Cancel button; add a fixed-price-per-period billing option for recurring jobs. The highest-value single item is to **wire up the seasonal and per-event snow modes that already exist in the schema**, with an instalment schedule. Leapfrog: we already have seasons, storms with pushes, trigger depths, auto-skip when depth changes, pause and resume, renewals and seven skip reasons, none of which the Jobber pages document. Cheap additions on top are depth-tiered pricing on one plan (Jobber needs a separate job per tier), a return-visit type linked to the storm and flagged billable or included, estimated against actual on-site minutes per property, and per-season contract profitability, which Jobber's recurring jobs cannot report. Skip multi-property jobs, standalone forms with conditional logic, chemical tracking and subcontractor assignment.

## Scheduling: close to Jobber's board, short on client control and configurability

**What we have.** A FullCalendar month, week, day and list calendar with drag and drop, an unscheduled drawer, a team board with a day map, "find a time" ranked by straight-line distance, bulk reschedule with an optional client email, working hours, a real time-off object, conflict warnings, repeating tasks and events, a day-before reminder with a Confirm link, and a free-text reschedule request. Gaps: no per-person timeline view, four hard-coded arrival windows that are not stored on the visit, no calendar feed, one fixed reminder at 5pm the day before, and a client who asks to reschedule cannot pick a new time. The team board uses native drag events, so it is probably desktop-only (untested).

**What Jobber does in depth.** The New Schedule's Day view is a per-person column board or a horizontal timeline, with map and unscheduled drawers ([Day view](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/36603691932951-Day-View-of-the-Schedule-New-Schedule)). "Find a Time" (Grow and Plus) highlights gaps and removes slots that cannot fit drive time, using turn-by-turn routes and historical traffic ([Schedule overview](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/schedule-overview-new-schedule/)). Up to two reminders per visit can be timed relative to the appointment or at a fixed clock time, and clients confirm by replying "Yes" or "C" to the text ([Assessment and visit reminders](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/360033608974-Assessment-and-Visit-Reminders)). Arrival windows are configurable from 15 minutes to 4 hours but are set per job and unavailable for anytime visits ([Arrival windows](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/arrival-windows/)). Calendar sync is a one-way subscribed feed ([Calendar syncing](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/115009378687-Calendar-Syncing)). Jobber is weaker than us in four documented places: time off is modelled as a task; bulk reschedule does not notify clients; conflicts only warn, as ours do; and client self-reschedule exists only through the paid AI Receptionist ([Receptionist](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/receptionistpowered-by-jobber-ai/)). The absence of a reschedule button in the client hub is inferred from the articles read, not stated by Jobber.

**What competitors do beyond Jobber.** Workiz's Genius Scheduling picks the time and technician from availability, proximity, skills and service area ([Workiz Genius Scheduling](https://www.workiz.com/features/genius-scheduling/)). ServiceM8 parks jobs in "waiting on" queues with an expiry date that resurfaces them on the dispatch board ([ServiceM8 queues](https://support.servicem8.com/help-center/servicem8-add-ons/servicem8-add-ons/queues-overview.md)). SingleOps batch-reschedules for weather and notifies customers ([Aspire roundup](https://www.youraspire.com/blog/best-snow-removal-business-apps)).

**Recommendations.** Parity: make arrival windows configurable and store the window on the visit; allow two reminders with configurable timing and reply-to-confirm by text; publish a one-way ICS feed per person; make the team board work by touch; add a per-person day timeline only if the crew grows past three or four. Use drive time rather than straight-line distance in find-a-time once a road-distance source exists (see routes). Leapfrog: a self-serve reschedule page where the client picks from real open slots reuses our slot engine and goes past what the Jobber hub documents; a "waiting on weather or thaw" queue with a resurface date suits yard work. Skip skills and equipment matching, vehicles, capacity planning and two-way Google or Outlook sync.

## Routes: a standing order by community beats Jobber's memory but not its maths

**What we have.** "Auto-order" groups properties by Calgary community, walks communities in a fixed seed order out from the depot, then runs nearest-neighbour and 2-opt inside each using **straight-line distance**, and writes a standing `routeSort` per property. Any day's route is that day's visits in that order. There is a split between people by stop count, a Leaflet map, a printable sheet, a Google Maps hand-off for the next four stops, a one-stop-at-a-time phone runner with an offline queue, and on-my-way texts that can fire automatically when the previous stop is finished. There are no drive times, no ETA anywhere, no route line, no per-person start or end point, no drag-to-reorder, no UI for community order, and no per-day override: nudging a stop changes every future day. Location is captured only on Arrived and Done.

**What Jobber does in depth.** The New Schedule optimiser reorders anytime visits using actual driving distance, for one or more people over up to seven days, with "keep first" and "keep last" as the only start and end control; it is admin-only, web-only, and the app cannot reorder ([Route optimization](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/route-optimization-new-schedule/)). The legacy account-wide master route, the closest thing to our standing order, is not available to accounts created from 12 November 2025 ([Route optimization through settings](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/115009785148-Route-Optimization-Through-the-Settings-Menu)). Jobber records GPS waypoints only on actions such as timers and completion, and states that live location is not shared ([GPS waypoint tracking](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/115009612507-GPS-Waypoint-Tracking)). Its manual on-my-way text carries a chosen ETA of 5 to 60 minutes ([On My Way](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/7448087796631-On-My-Way-Text-Messages-in-the-Jobber-App)). Review snippets say snow operators want reusable fixed routes; those snippets and the forum complaints about "as the crow flies" routing could not be opened or dated, and the latter probably predate the current optimiser.

**What competitors do beyond Jobber.** Snow tools build routes once and launch them per storm: SnowBossPro's "Start a Storm" sends all routes at once and builds a route from a circle drawn on the map, and Plowdesk, Plow360, Icey and Yeti all centre on saved storm routes with a live progress board ([Upper roundup](https://www.upperinc.com/blog/best-snow-removal-business-software.md)). Skimmer separates one-time changes for a single day from permanent ones and lets start and end be the office or a tech's home ([Skimmer](https://www.getskimmer.com/product/technicians)); GorillaDesk asks whether an accepted route applies to "these specific jobs only" or all future recurring jobs ([GorillaDesk help](https://intercom.help/gorilladesk/en/articles/8400041-route-optimization)). OptimoRoute adds priorities, time windows and service duration; Upper treats time windows as preferences and pushes changes to drivers mid-route. Automatic "on the way" and "driveway cleared" texts are standard in the small snow tools ([SnowBossPro residential](https://snowbosspro.com/residential-snow-removal-software)).

**Recommendations.** Parity: replace haversine with a road-distance matrix (OSRM self-hosted or a metered API) inside the existing community-then-2-opt ordering, and draw the route line. Leapfrog, all cheap once drive times exist: a "today only or every time" choice on every reorder; drag-to-reorder on the list and map; a settings screen for community order; a start and end point per person; split by estimated time, not stop count; an ETA or "you are stop N of M" in storm and on-my-way texts; a priority flag that pins seniors or early commuters to the front. Our storm launch, standing order, automatic next-stop texts and completion message with photos already match what the snow specialists sell and exceed what Jobber documents. Skip a full vehicle-routing solver with capacities and hard time windows, live breadcrumb tracking, geofenced auto-arrival and vehicle telematics. Jobber gets vehicles only through paid hardware integrations, and its client-facing live tracking link is US only ([Force Fleet Tracking](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-and-force-fleet-tracking/)).

## Crew, permissions and time: every user is an owner today

**What we have.** `UserRole { OWNER, CREW }` exists but no code reads it, new team members are created as `OWNER`, and the only guard is `requireOwner`. Crew therefore see prices, billing and settings. Owner alerts go to an environment list, not to assigned staff. Time is a per-visit timer plus manual entries in a weekly grid, with an hourly cost per person feeding job profit. There is no general clock-in, no approval step, no export and no push notification.

**What Jobber does in depth.** Four additive presets (Field crew, Senior field crew, Crew lead, Manager) plus an admin flag and granular toggles. The default invite level hides pricing and other people's schedules, and client visibility can be cut to "name and address only (assigned jobs)" ([User permissions](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/115009568687-User-Permissions)). Timesheets have a general clock-in that pauses while a visit timer runs, a cutoff-date approval that locks entries, an edit history and an Excel export ([Timesheets](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/115009543028-Timesheets); [Approving timesheets](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/115009614247-Approving-Time-Sheets)). Geofenced location timers are Grow and above ([Location timers](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/14125014745239-Location-Timers-in-the-Jobber-App)). Crews, crew clock-in and break categories are Plus only ([Crew scheduling](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/crew-scheduling/)). Crew get push notifications when today's schedule changes ([Jobber app basics](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/7061327071639-Jobber-App-Basics)). No overtime or pay-period logic was found, and payroll sync runs through Gusto, a US payroll product, or QuickBooks Payroll.

**What competitors do beyond Jobber.** Skimmer forces photos to be taken in-app and blocks finishing a stop until required items are done ([Skimmer](https://www.getskimmer.com/product/technicians)); we already block on required checklist items, which is stricter than Jobber. Piece-rate pay, crew scores and equipment tracking were not confirmed for any product.

**Recommendations.** Parity, and first on the whole roadmap: enforce the `CREW` role. A crew login should see its own route and visits, no prices, no billing, no settings, and create no owners. Then send assignment and schedule-change alerts to the assigned person, add a general clock-in and out, and add timesheet approval with a CSV export. Web push for schedule changes fits the installed PWA. Skip payroll, overtime rules, a crews entity (useful only past five or so people), geofence timers, certifications and SSO.

## Invoicing and payments: ahead on e-Transfer, behind on cards on file and editing

**What we have.** Charge-based invoicing from six creation paths, a billing run that drafts for everyone and sends all, deposit invoices with automatic "Less deposit" credit, Stripe Checkout with fee capture and "Pay all", partial payments, bad debt, void, referral credits, receipts and statements. Interac e-Transfer is ingested from DKIM-verified bank emails, matched by `ETC-n` reference or amount and surname, with a scored manual queue and splits across three invoices. Missing: **card on file, auto-pay and any recurring billing**; refunds from inside the CRM; tips; late fees; invoice editing (no line edits even on drafts, `discountCents` always 0, no per-invoice terms or message); manual credits and overpayments; configurable reminders (fixed at two emails); real PDFs; any tax report. Stripe disputes and failed asynchronous payments are not handled, and the payments page totals only the latest 300 rows.

**What Jobber does in depth.** Batch create produces drafts per client and batch deliver sends them by email or as a print file with mailing labels ([Batch create](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/batch-create-invoices); [Batch deliver](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/batch-deliver-invoices)). Payment terms include Net 7 to Net 60 and custom, with a per-client override ([Payment terms](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/set-invoice-payment-terms)). Deposits and overpayments sit on a client account balance and can be applied to any invoice ([Client billing history](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/client-billing-history)). Refunds are issued in-app, full or partial ([Refunds](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-payments-refunds)). Tax is per property with tax groups, and a taxation report offers accrual and cash views ([Tax settings](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/tax-settings); [Taxation report](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/taxation-report)). Cards are saved in a client wallet and recurring jobs can charge automatically at month end, after each visit or on a custom schedule, on Connect and above in Canada ([Automatic payments](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/automatic-payments)). Tips are offered at 10, 15 and 20 percent and default on for snow removal and lawn care ([Tip collection](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/tip-collection-with-jobber-payments)). A disputes dashboard exists ([Disputes](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-payments-disputes)).

Jobber's weak spots matter as much. A failed automatic charge is tried once with no retry. The researcher found no computed late-fee or interest feature, only a "late stamp" and disclaimer text; the one direct statement is an undated community thread, so treat this as likely rather than certain ([Jobber Community](https://community.getjobber.com/discussions/finances-forum/auto-add-late-feesinterest/917)). No surcharge feature was documented. And Jobber has **no native e-Transfer collection**: "E-transfer" appears only as a manually recorded offline method ([Deposits on quotes](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/deposits-on-quotes/)).

**What competitors do beyond Jobber.** Skimmer adds failed-payment customer alerts with a daily failed-transaction report, prepaid credit balances that later invoices draw down, and bulk invoice generation with a preview step ([Skimmer](https://www.getskimmer.com/product/technicians)). Housecall Pro charges any outstanding balance immediately when a customer updates the card on a plan ([HCP service plans FAQs](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/5485025-service-plans-faqs)).

**Recommendations.** Parity: draft invoice editing with a discount, per-invoice terms and message; card on file through Stripe with auto-charge for seasonal instalments, monthly recurring invoices and per-push storm invoices; refunds from the CRM; manual credits, overpayments and deposits held on account; configurable reminder timing with an SMS option; real PDFs for invoices, quotes and statements with a statement email; aged receivables and a GST collected report; tips on the pay page; selection in "send all drafts"; handling for Stripe disputes and failed payments; fixing the 300-row totals. Leapfrog: keep e-Transfer as the default rail and extend it, for example by showing the `ETC-n` reference and a copyable payee on every invoice and reminder; add retry and a client-facing failure message on auto-pay, which Jobber's single attempt lacks; an optional late-fee rule would go past Jobber but is low priority for residential clients. Skip ACH-style bank debit, consumer financing, card readers and progress-invoicing schedules beyond the seasonal instalment plan.

## Communications, automations and marketing: ahead on texting, fixed in code

**What we have.** Two-way SMS and MMS with RCS buttons and SMS fallback, a shared inbox, STOP and START handling, about 25 transactional emails and nine SMS templates, email and SMS campaigns with CASL consent logic, quiet hours and tracking, a rating-first review flow, and a referral program with credits. Eleven automations are on/off switches. **All wording is in code**: `EmailTemplateOverride` exists as a model but is unused, and there is no logo or branding setting. No bounce or complaint handling exists. Campaigns have no automated or triggered sends. Quick replies are four hard-coded strings.

**What Jobber does in depth.** Every template is editable with about 40 merge variables ([Variables](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/variables)), and document branding (logo, header colour, layout) is set once ([Client document settings](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/client-document-settings)). A communications report shows sent, failed or bounced, and opened ([Client communications report](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/client-communications-report)). Two-way texting is Grow and above ([Two-way text messaging](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/two-way-text-messaging)). Campaigns are **email only**, with one-off and automated templates such as win-back and "close on pending quotes", and revenue attribution to jobs created within 30 days ([Campaigns](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/campaigns-marketing-tools)). Review requests allow a choice of trigger, a six-month cap and two follow-ups, and auto-skip clients who appear to have already reviewed ([Reviews](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/reviews-marketing-tools)). Campaigns, reviews and referral rewards need Plus or the US$99 Marketing Suite. The custom automation builder is narrow: it acts only on quotes and invoices, with date-offset triggers up to 90 days and three actions (notify client, add tag, archive quote) ([Custom automation builder](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/custom-automation-builder/)); sources conflict on whether it is Grow or Plus. The AI Receptionist answers calls and texts, books through online-booking rules, takes messages into tasks and texts back callers who hang up; it is available in Canada at US$99 a month for 100 conversations or included in Plus ([Receptionist](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/receptionistpowered-by-jobber-ai/)).

**What competitors do beyond Jobber.** Housecall Pro's automation primitive is a starting status, a delay, optional conditions and one action, with quiet hours for texts ([HCP Pipeline](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6185127-getting-started-with-pipeline)). Housecall Pro, FieldPulse and ServiceM8 bundle an AI writing helper in base plans ([ServiceM8 pricing](https://www.servicem8.com/pricing)). Skimmer sends a "Skipped stop" text alongside on-my-way and finished ([Skimmer](https://www.getskimmer.com/product/technicians)).

**Recommendations.** Parity: wire up `EmailTemplateOverride` so owners can edit email and SMS wording, and add logo and colour settings; write `emailBouncedAt` from provider bounce and complaint webhooks; make reminder and follow-up timings settings, not constants; add review follow-ups and a trigger choice; add two or three automated campaigns (win-back after N months, renewal before the season, pending-quote nudge) and let scheduled campaigns be cancelled or duplicated; make quick replies editable. Leapfrog: we already send SMS campaigns with consent handling where Jobber's are email only, and we already notify on visit completion with photos, which Jobber's notification list does not include. Cheap additions are a missed-call text-back and a message-to-task flow on the Telnyx number (the low-cost core of an AI receptionist), a "skipped" client text, and a storm broadcast with route position. Skip a general workflow builder: Jobber's own is limited to quotes and invoices, and configurable timings on our existing automations cover the same ground. Also skip an AI voice receptionist, social posting, a website builder, competitor review analysis and per-user phone numbers for now.

## Reporting and platform: one fixed page, no accounting bridge, thin test cover

**What we have.** A home dashboard with workflow cards and eleven to-do counters, one fixed Reports page (money in by month, visits, approval rate, hours by person, profit by job, invoiced by service, money in by community, storm table) and three CSV exports. No date pickers, filters or drill-down. Lists do not paginate: requests, quotes and invoices load 1,000 rows and clients and jobs load everything. There is no accounting integration, no API or webhooks, and settings are thin. The PWA's service worker caches page HTML only, checklists, timers, signatures and payments need a connection, and there are no browser or end-to-end tests.

**What Jobber does in depth.** About 30 reports grouped as financial, work and client, each with date ranges, a column picker and CSV by email, including aged receivables, taxation, projected income, client balance summary, lead source, timesheets and team productivity ([Reports basics](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/reports-basics); [Invoice and payment reports](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/invoice-and-payment-reports)). A free Report Scheduler emails selected reports on a schedule ([Report scheduler](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/report-scheduler-app)). One-way sync to QuickBooks Online and Xero is available in Canada on Connect and above ([QuickBooks sync](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/how-items-sync-between-jobber-and-quickbooks-online-new-quickbooks-integration); [Xero](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-and-xero-integration)). Zapier exposes 11 triggers and 6 actions ([Zapier](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-and-zapier-integration)). Offline support for forms, visit details, notes and timers arrived in early 2026 ([Jobber product updates](https://www.getjobber.com/product-updates/)). Jobber's developer documentation returned 403, so API and webhook details are second-hand.

**What competitors do beyond Jobber.** Housecall Pro's Analyst AI answers reporting questions in plain language and saves the result as a dashboard tile, and its service line item report shows margin per service within a job ([HCP fall 2025 product updates](https://housecallpro.com/resources/fall-2025-product-updates)). One comparison site rates Jobber's reporting above Housecall Pro's, without sourcing, so parity with Jobber is a sufficient target here.

**Recommendations.** Parity: date ranges on the Reports page; aged receivables, GST collected, client balances, lead source and timesheet reports; CSV export for clients, jobs, quotes, invoices and time; list pagination; a monthly accountant export (invoices, payments, GST) before any live QuickBooks or Xero sync, which can come later if the bookkeeper asks. Leapfrog: per-season, per-contract and per-community profitability, which Jobber does not report for recurring work. Platform hardening that the inventory flags and that matters in a storm: cache the app shell properly for cold starts with no signal, stop deleting queued photos on a 401, move the booking rate limit out of process memory, and add a browser test for the booking, quote approval, payment and runner flows. Skip a public API, a Zapier app, a native mobile app, multi-business tenancy and an AI report builder.

## Canada: what is US-only in Jobber, and where Interac and GST apply

Several Jobber payment and integration features do not reach a Calgary business, which shrinks the parity target. **US only:** ACH bank payments ([Bank payments](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/bank-payments-ach)); Wisetack consumer financing, which also lists snow removal as an excluded industry ([Wisetack](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-and-wisetack-consumer-financing-integration)); cheque scan and Jobber Money; Gusto payroll ([Gusto](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/jobber-and-gusto-integration)); Force Fleet Tracking and its live tracking links; the Home Depot catalogue; Google AI Mode. A2P 10DLC registration is a US-only step ([Dedicated phone number](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/dedicated-phone-number)).

**Available in Canada with differences:** Tap to Pay does not support Interac debit, and taps over $250 may need a reader or manual entry ([Tap to Pay](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/tap-to-pay)). Instant payouts need a Visa or Mastercard debit card, not Interac ([Instant payouts](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/instant-payouts)). Canadian accounts accept card only in the client hub. Available as in the US: Jobber Payments, automatic payments, texting, the AI Receptionist, drive-time booking rules, FleetSharp vehicle tracking, QuickBooks Online and Xero sync. Canadian card rates and plan prices in CAD were not confirmed.

**Interac.** Jobber records e-Transfers by hand. Our DKIM-verified auto-matching with a manual queue is a genuine lead on the payment rail most Canadian residential clients prefer, and we absorb no card fee on it. Protect that lead before adding card features.

**GST.** We support one rate (default GST 5%), registered or not, tax-inclusive or on top. That is enough for Alberta. The gaps are that quotes never compute tax, there is no GST collected report for filing, expenses carry no GST split for input tax credits, and there is no per-line or per-client exemption. Jobber's tax groups exist for combined-rate provinces ([Tax settings](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/tax-settings)); we do not need them unless the business works outside Alberta.

**Bylaw timer.** Calgary's Street Bylaw requires owners or occupants to clear the bordering sidewalk within 24 hours after snowfall ends ([City of Calgary](https://www.calgary.ca/bylaws/snow-shovelling.html)). The specialist researcher found no product with a municipal deadline timer. Recording "snowfall ended at" on a storm, showing a countdown, ranking unfinished stops by deadline risk and stamping "cleared before deadline" on the completion notice is a small build on our storm object. Fine amounts ($250, $500, $750) come from a single news snippet and are not confirmed on a City page, so do not print them in client messages yet.

## Caveats the researchers asked to carry through

No Reddit, PlowSite or LawnSite threads were retrieved by any researcher. Claims about what small operators want are inferred from product positioning, pricing and a few review-site snippets, not from practitioner testimony, and a follow-up pass on those forums is needed before treating the priorities above as validated by operators. Most competitor detail is vendor marketing or vendor help pages, and several "best snow software" roundups were written by vendors who rank themselves first. Competitor prices conflict between sources and should be checked before quoting.

Three Jobber sources were blocked: developer.getjobber.com, productupdates.getjobber.com and the app marketplace all returned 403. API and webhook details, release dates in 2025 and 2026, and the list of marketplace apps (including any purchasing or inventory apps) are therefore second-hand or missing. Several help articles say only "select plans", so some plan gating is approximate.

Statements that Jobber lacks something are mostly "not documented in the pages read": automatic late fees, card surcharges, quote expiry, client self-reschedule in the hub, live crew tracking, duplicate detection, pause or skip on recurring jobs, and photos or reports in the client hub. Quote Options is known only from a search snippet. Likewise, "no product has it" for bylaw timers, contract caps, return visits, renewals, waitlists and self-serve add-ons means no evidence was found, not that it does not exist. Zuper, Method CRM, Markate, Attentive and SiteRecon were not meaningfully covered. Whether any certified weather service covers Calgary was not established.

## Conclusion

The audit changes the shape of the problem. The areas flagged as "very basic" are not uniformly behind: ordering materials is an area where Jobber itself has almost nothing, routing is one where we kept the standing route Jobber removed but skipped the road maths, and the CRM is a rich record missing its tools (import, merge, pipeline) more than its data. What actually separates us from Jobber is that Jobber lets a user change anything after the fact and lets the owner configure everything without a developer, while our records freeze at creation and our wording, timings, windows and service list live in code.

That points to an order of work different from "build the missing modules". Finish what the schema already promises, make records and settings editable, close the access-control hole, then spend the larger builds on the three places a Calgary snow crew gains most: auto-charged seasonal plans, routes that know the roads and the day, and a hub where a client can buy something. The open risk is validation. Before committing to the larger builds, a short pass through operator forums and a week of the crew's own storm-day friction would test priorities that today rest on vendor pages and a code read.
